Every Friday my inbox fills up with "huge AI launches." Most of them don't matter to a bakery, a cleaning company, or a solo consultant. A few do. This week, one of them is something you can act on in about 20 minutes, for free.

Here's what happened between November 10 and November 20, 2026, what it means for a small business, and what you can safely ignore.

## Why this week matters

For two years, the question has been "Does ChatGPT even know my business exists?" On Tuesday I wrote about [how to check whether ChatGPT recommends your business](/ai-visibility/does-chatgpt-recommend-your-business/). This week, OpenAI gave business owners an official way to manage how they show up. That's new. Google, Anthropic and Microsoft also shipped tools, but most of them sit behind paid or business plans.

## The 4 stories that matter this week

### 1. ChatGPT now lets businesses claim their profile (beta)

On November 10, OpenAI introduced **Businesses in ChatGPT**, in beta ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/introducing-businesses-chatgpt/)). It has two parts:

- **Business Hub:** you claim your business, manage its information (think of it like a Google Business Profile, but for ChatGPT), and see insights.
- **Business Agents:** a separate, labeled chat where ChatGPT answers customer questions on your behalf. For now this is only for **eligible ecommerce and online service businesses in the United States** with a website.

What you see in insights: total mentions, website clicks, and the topics people were asking about. You **don't** see individual conversations. OpenAI's help page says insights cover the past 28 days ([OpenAI Help](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001635-setting-up-your-business-on-chatgpt)).

Two details worth knowing:

- "You don't need an Ads account or a ChatGPT Enterprise agreement to claim an eligible business," says OpenAI.
- Claiming alone does **not** give you a verified badge.

**Cost:** 🆓 OpenAI lists no fee to claim. Business Agents are limited to eligible US online businesses.

### 2. Google launched Gemini 4 Neon

On November 18, Google started rolling out **Gemini 4 Neon**, a model built for deeper analysis at faster speed, with a 1-million-token context window ([Google](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/introducing-gemini-4-neon/)). A "token" is a small piece of text, so 1 million tokens means it can read very long documents at once.

In the Gemini app, Neon is for **Google AI Pro and Ultra** subscribers. Developers pay $3 per million input tokens and $15 per million output tokens through the Gemini API.

**Cost:** 💳 Paid. Google AI Pro is $19.99/month in the US. Free alternative: the free Gemini app, with lower limits and Google's everyday model.

### 3. Claude can now join your Google Meet calls (beta)

Anthropic launched **Claude Meetings** in beta on November 12 ([Claude](https://claude.com/resources/articles/claude-meetings)). Claude joins a video call as a visible participant, takes notes, and can build documents and whiteboards while you talk. A few things I like from a privacy point of view:

- It stays out of calls with outside guests by default.
- It saves a transcript and summary, **not audio**.
- It only works with **Google Meet** for now.

Two days earlier, Anthropic also released **Claude Sheets** and **Claude Whiteboards** (both beta, on all paid plans) and made Claude Data generally available ([Claude](https://claude.com/resources/articles/sheets-whiteboards-data)).

**Cost:** 💳 Paid. Claude Meetings is only on **Team and Enterprise** plans. Team starts at $25 per seat per month billed monthly ($20 billed annually), with a 2-seat minimum. Sheets and Whiteboards are on any paid plan, including Pro at $20/month ($17/month billed annually) ([Claude pricing](https://claude.com/pricing)). During the beta, docs and whiteboards made in a call are free through February 10, 2027. Free alternative: the free Claude plan, or Google Docs plus your own notes.

### 4. Microsoft Ignite put Word, Excel and PowerPoint inside Copilot

At Microsoft Ignite (November 17–20, San Francisco), Microsoft said **Office in Copilot**, which brings Word, Excel and PowerPoint fully into Copilot, is now generally available ([Microsoft](https://news.microsoft.com/events/ignite/2026/live-news/)). It also showed a morning briefing called "Today" (private preview in Outlook and the Copilot app) and a proactive agent called Autopilot, rolling out to its early-access "Frontier" customers.

**Cost:** 💳 Paid. Microsoft's announcement page doesn't list plan prices, and most of these features target larger companies. Free alternative: the free Microsoft Copilot app for everyday questions.

**One more headline:** Anthropic, the company behind Claude, priced its initial public offering on November 18 ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/anthropic-prices-initial-public-offering)). For your day-to-day work, nothing changes. This is not investment advice.

## The opportunity: claim your business in ChatGPT

This is this week's one free, practical move. Here's how, based on OpenAI's help page:

1. **Sign in to ChatGPT** and open **Business Hub** from the sidebar.
2. **Find your business** and choose how to verify it: business phone (text or call), business email, website domain (a TXT record, which is a small line of text you or your web person add to your domain settings), or your Shopify store.
3. **Wait for the approval email** from OpenAI.
4. **Verify your identity** through Persona, OpenAI's verification partner. You need this before you can publish changes, see insights, or create an agent.
5. **Fill in your information** carefully and make it match your website and Google Business Profile.

### Prep checklist (have this ready before you start)

- [ ] Exact business name, the same everywhere
- [ ] Phone and email you can receive codes on
- [ ] Hours, service area, and address (if you have a public one)
- [ ] A one-sentence description of what you do and who you serve
- [ ] Your top 5 services or products, in plain words
- [ ] Your website link

### Copy-paste prompt to write your description

```
I own a small business. Help me write a clear, honest business description
for my ChatGPT business profile.

Business name: [name]
What we do: [services/products]
Who we serve: [type of customer]
Where: [city/area or "online"]
What makes us different: [1-2 real facts, no hype]
Languages we serve customers in: [e.g., English and Spanish]

Write:
1. A 1-sentence description (under 25 words)
2. A 3-sentence description
3. A list of 8 plain-language phrases customers might use to search for us
Don't invent awards, years, prices, or claims I didn't give you.
```

A second option, if you like learning in person: Inc. reports that Bluehost's free **AI Builder Tour** for small businesses has stops in New York City on December 3 and Chicago on January 12 ([Inc.](https://www.inc.com/aaron-mok/small-business-owners-can-get-a-free-crash-course-on-ai-from-google-and-tiktok-heres-how/91432381)). It's a hosting company's marketing event, so expect a sales pitch alongside the lessons.

## The honest truth

- **Claiming your profile is not a ranking guarantee.** It helps ChatGPT get your facts right. It doesn't promise that ChatGPT will recommend you over anyone else.
- **It's a beta.** Features, rules, and who qualifies can change. You can't manage many locations from one place yet, and agents can't complete purchases or bookings.
- **You'll share identity information** with a verification company. Read what you're agreeing to before you upload anything.
- **New models are not a reason to switch tools.** If your free Gemini, ChatGPT or Claude plan does the job, keep it. Upgrade only when you hit a real limit.
- **Your basics still matter more.** A complete Google Business Profile, real reviews, and a clear website feed every AI tool, not just ChatGPT.

## Your one action for today

Open ChatGPT, click **Business Hub**, and see whether your business is listed. If it is, start the claim. If you only have 5 minutes, just fill out the checklist above so you're ready.

## La oportunidad: reclama tu negocio en ChatGPT

Este es el paso práctico y gratuito de la semana. Así se hace, según la página de ayuda de OpenAI:

1. **Inicia sesión en ChatGPT** y abre **Business Hub** desde la barra lateral.
2. **Busca tu negocio** y elige cómo verificarlo: teléfono del negocio (mensaje de texto o llamada), correo del negocio, dominio de tu sitio web (un registro TXT, que es una pequeña línea de texto que tú o la persona que maneja tu web agregan en la configuración del dominio) o tu tienda de Shopify.
3. **Espera el correo de aprobación** de OpenAI.
4. **Verifica tu identidad** con Persona, la empresa de verificación que usa OpenAI. La necesitas antes de publicar cambios, ver estadísticas o crear un agente.
5. **Llena tu información** con cuidado y haz que coincida con tu sitio web y tu Perfil de Negocio de Google.

### Lista para prepararte (tenla lista antes de empezar)

- [ ] Nombre exacto del negocio, igual en todas partes
- [ ] Teléfono y correo donde puedas recibir códigos
- [ ] Horario, zona que atiendes y dirección (si tienes una pública)
- [ ] Una oración que diga qué haces y a quién atiendes
- [ ] Tus 5 servicios o productos principales, en palabras sencillas
- [ ] El enlace de tu sitio web

### Prompt para copiar y pegar y escribir tu descripción

```
Tengo un negocio pequeño. Ayúdame a escribir una descripción clara y honesta
para el perfil de mi negocio en ChatGPT.

Nombre del negocio: [nombre]
Qué hacemos: [servicios/productos]
A quién atendemos: [tipo de cliente]
Dónde: [ciudad/zona o "en línea"]
Qué nos hace diferentes: [1 o 2 datos reales, sin exagerar]
Idiomas en los que atendemos: [por ejemplo, inglés y español]

Escribe:
1. Una descripción de 1 oración (menos de 25 palabras)
2. Una descripción de 3 oraciones
3. Una lista de 8 frases sencillas que los clientes podrían usar para buscarnos
No inventes premios, años, precios ni afirmaciones que yo no te haya dado.
```

## La verdad, sin rodeos

- **Reclamar tu perfil no garantiza posiciones.** Ayuda a que ChatGPT tenga bien tus datos. No promete que ChatGPT te recomiende por encima de otros.
- **Es una beta.** Las funciones, las reglas y quién califica pueden cambiar. Todavía no puedes manejar varias sucursales desde un solo lugar, y los agentes no pueden completar compras ni reservas.
- **Vas a compartir datos de identidad** con una empresa de verificación. Lee lo que estás aceptando antes de subir cualquier documento.
- **Un modelo nuevo no es razón para cambiar de herramienta.** Si tu plan gratis de Gemini, ChatGPT o Claude te sirve, quédate con él. Paga solo cuando llegues a un límite real.
- **Lo básico sigue pesando más.** Un Perfil de Negocio de Google completo, reseñas reales y un sitio web claro alimentan a todas las herramientas de IA, no solo a ChatGPT.
